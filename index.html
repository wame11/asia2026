Korea is harder than Japan and I won't pretend otherwise. But temple cuisine isn't a compromise — it's a 1,500-year-old refined tradition, and Insadong has the best concentration of it in the country. Between that, kimbap on every corner, Gwangjang's vegetable mayak gimbap and Chabad as a backstop, you will eat very well.

📎 Money, temples & the honest notes

💷 MoneyJapan is 85–90% card · Korea more so · both have cash-only markets

🇯🇵 Japan — mostly card, but not entirely

Contactless Visa/Mastercard plus a Suica covers 85–90% — all trains, every konbini, most restaurants and hotels. The other 10–15% is small family-run restaurants, temples, market stalls (Tsukiji is cash-heavy) and vending machines.

  • 💴 Carry ¥15,000–20,000 (~£70–95) per adult
  • 🏧 Top up at 7-Eleven or Japan Post ATMs — they take foreign cards and beat the exchange desks
  • 🚫 Don't change money at the airport counter

🇰🇷 Korea — even more cashless

Card works almost everywhere including buses and taxis. Except traditional markets — Gwangjang is largely cash. Carry ₩50,000–100,000 (~£27–55) on market days.

🗣️ Three questions, answered straightOsaka · swimming · Korean food

🏯 On Kyoto vs Osaka. Kyoto, no Osaka — and I'd have said that even without Jidai Matsuri. Two nights is a day and a half. Osaka is a 15-minute train ride but a whole day of attention you don't have, and its food scene, the actual reason to go, is largely octopus and pork.

🚫 On freshwater swimming. Nothing worth doing. Late-October lake and river water in Japan and Korea sits around 12–16°C with no lifeguarded swims running. Any option would be a long cold journey for a bad swim. The Sheraton's heated 25m indoor pool is the swimming on this trip.

🍽️ On Korean food. Harder than Japan, and temple cuisine is the answer — see the food section.
